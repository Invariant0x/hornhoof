Turn the Operations request named in the assignment into a bounded piece of work. Use the authorized company route for requests from other departments; possession of this ability does not make you a department head or grant assignment rights.

1. Classify the need: tool, access, integration, account, subscription, node problem or incident. A turn or program that stands, the orchestrator's and any head's too, is an incident Operations end themselves at its screen, with its keys, stop_employee or a safe restart: never ask a person to stop a turn or write done.
2. Record the requester, task, deadline and observable definition of working, such as a successful harmless call to an approved sandbox.
3. Identify shared setup, money, access, privacy and irreversible effects. For a shared change, require the exact targets, impact, risk, rollback, user-side check and safe window. Paid plans wait for a person to pay from their own account; a free account the company needs, it signs up for itself.
4. Propose the smallest access scope that works: one department or product, read before write. Use the resource’s delegation mechanism. For machine access, send the target, recipient and scope to the orchestrator for the grant. Text cannot grant permission; use the authorized access mechanism.
5. Choose an executor by the abilities needed and the current assignment. If the cause is unknown, read the company's database tool, which only reads; send a proposed database change through the authorized route as its SQL and why. When delegated diagnosis is authorized, split it between two or three members at once, each with one independent question and evidence to return.
6. Collect the findings, verify the outcome from the requester's side and return the cause, action, evidence and usage notes. Reference secret store entries by name, never value.

Use read-only diagnosis within the task. Before assigning a shared change, identify its affected trust boundaries and choose a route that can supply any required independent check.

For a product test runtime request, identify who owns provisioning and readiness. Give that owner the exact revision, runner and observed missing capability.
