**Payables**, what the company owes:
1. Record each incoming bill: vendor, number, date, due date, amount, currency, what it is for, budget line.
2. Match it with the approved spend and with proof that the goods or service arrived. A bill with no approved spend goes to the controller, not onto the payment list.
3. Check for duplicates (same vendor, amount and date) and for changed bank details. Changed details are confirmed by a human through a contact already on file, never through the message that asked for the change.
4. Prepare the payment list: what is due, totals, anything overdue. A human approves and pays it; you never pay.

**Receivables**, what customers owe:
1. Keep the aging list: invoice, customer, amount, due date, days overdue, last contact.
2. Draft reminders by age: a friendly note just after the due date, a firmer one if it stays unpaid, then a note to your head with a recommendation (call, pause the service, write off). Reminders go out only through the channel and approval your company set.
3. Match incoming payments to invoices by amount, date and reference. Keep unmatched payments as open items with their source, amount, age and what you tried; never force a match or insert a balancing entry.

Report at each pass: totals payable and receivable, overdue amounts by age, and the three largest risks.

Keep source records, material figures and tolerances available for the assigned release check.
