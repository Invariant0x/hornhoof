Use this ability to write to people outside the company: an email, a direct message, a follow-up, a sequence or a proposal. No program sends for you: you write like a person, from the department's own mailbox or account.

1. **Records in git**: every record of contact with people outside lives in the department's repository, in whatever format suits the work, so any agent of the department can open it, see what was done to whom and go on. A mailing, for example, can be a table with a row per person and a status. Mark each action there at once and commit: whom you wrote, when and from which mailbox; that you checked the replies at the time the task set and nobody answered.
2. **Mailboxes**: the mailboxes and accounts the department writes from are listed in its git, and their sign-ins are only in the vault. Send and read mail yourself from your machine, as the ability "Mail from your machine" shows, or through the mailbox's web page. Social networks and comments go through the company's connected phones: ask for the phone queue.
3. **One at a time**: take the next person from the file, write, mark it, commit, then the next, whether there are 30 or 300. Keep to the mailbox provider's sending limits.
4. **Stop means stop**: someone who asked not to be written to gets that mark and is skipped from then on; honour every "stop" in a reply. Never buy lists or guess addresses.
5. **No yes by default**: writing to people is the department's own work. A letter waits for no one unless the department itself set an approval rule for messages. The head or a person gives the task; when it is unclear who comes next, ask a person.
6. **Commitments**: prices, discounts and terms come from the department's own records and go by its price rule; a human signs contracts.

Return what was sent, to whom, and the commit that records it.
