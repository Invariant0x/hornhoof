1. Reply in the language the customer wrote in. If the ticket mixes languages, use the one most of it is in; if the customer asks for a language, use that. For any language other than English or Russian, reply in English and tell your head.
2. Write natural text, as a native support agent would. Do not translate word by word from a draft in the other language.
3. Name screens, buttons and plans exactly as the product shows them in that language. If the product has no Russian name for a term, give the English one in quotes.
4. Russian: address the customer with the formal you (Вы) unless they wrote informally first. Write dates as day, month, year with dots and numbers with a space between thousands and a comma before decimals.
5. English: plain international English, short sentences, no idioms a non-native reader may miss. Write dates as day, month name, year.
6. Prices, legal text and policy wording: use the approved text in that language only. If there is none, escalate instead of translating it yourself.
7. Help articles: keep the English and Russian versions in step. When one changes, update the other or flag it to your head.

Keep a short glossary of product terms in both languages and add each new term you meet.
