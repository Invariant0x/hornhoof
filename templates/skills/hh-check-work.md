You can check a result against its task and hand it forward only when it meets it, at a checking stage of its route or on work that is yours from start to end.

Read "task" first: its body says what was asked and how it is accepted, and task.echelons names your stage and the candidate you check. You check the result, not its author's account of it. The check is the same for your own result: read it cold, as if another had written it.

At a one-shot review stage, follow the ability "One-shot calls". In the brief, give each call an independent check with the candidate, its acceptance criteria and the evidence needed for that check. Use the route's judge to combine the findings. Verify the findings you rely on against the sources, and do checks that need live access yourself before giving your verdict.

**What to read.** Where the result is a commit, read what it changed from task.review. Where it is a document, read the hand-in in the task log and fetch every file it names with `hh-fetch`. Read the sources the result relies on, not only the result.

1. **Does it do what the task asked?** Every requirement met, nothing added that was not asked. Missing work is a finding.
2. **Is each claim true?** Take every number, name, date, price, quote and link to its source and compare. Rebuild a total from its source records instead of reading the author's workings. A claim with no source is a finding.
3. **Is it fit for whom it reaches?** The language, tone and form the task names. Nothing private, and nothing promised that nobody approved.
4. **The department's own rules.** The other skills on your card say what else a result of this kind must meet, such as a brand voice, an approval limit or a support policy. Apply them.
5. **Protected steps.** Paying, signing in the company's name and deleting for good keep a person's own yes: a payment and a signature are a person's, and an irreversible deletion needs the CEO's yes. Your PASS never replaces that yes. Publishing, sending and posting are the department's own work and wait for nobody's yes unless the department set an approval rule for them.

**Each finding** has a place (the file and its line or section), what is wrong and the harm to a person. Check it against the source before you record it: a wrong finding costs its reader a round.

**What comes next.** Read the route for the repair owner. When the check requires an independent verdict, preserve the candidate and send findings to the repair owner. When the stage assigns repairs to you, record your changes and get any independent check required on the repaired version. Give the verdict for the exact version you actually checked. Escalate a wrong goal to the head. On an end-to-end assignment correct your own work and record the result.
