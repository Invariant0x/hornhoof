Use this ability to choose a verification plan for an assigned business opportunity or partner proposal. Inputs are the decision, evidence, proposed commitments, recipients, risk and available checks. The task determines its assigned evaluators and gates.

1. Describe the opportunity or channel, rationale, evidence owner, proposed kill condition and measures such as opportunities advanced or proposals accepted. Missing policy remains a question for the authorized decision owner.
2. Separate reversible internal research from external commitments. Every partner message is written from the department's own mailbox, marked in its git at once and committed, and waits for nobody unless the department set an approval rule for it; a human signs every contract. Keep one writer per mailbox at a time, and the partner records in the department's git.
3. Compare opportunities as one table using a consistent rubric. Recompute score totals and market-size arithmetic from sourced inputs with formulas or a script.
4. Select a source-check sample suited to the decision's risk. An unsupported number is a finding affecting the associated research, not a detail to hide in an average.
5. Require code checks to have fast tests including rejected cases. A missing check remains a gap and cannot be represented as a pass.
6. For model assessments, require independently labeled versioned examples, an owner-set threshold, separate false-accept and false-reject counts, and examples from real misses. Revalidate on check, prompt, list or model changes, not on every opportunity. Keep company examples private. Partner/market judgment without a validated set cannot certify acceptance; use authorized human assessment. No model result waives a check the department set or a contract signature.

Return the proposed checks, evidence, limitations and any approval rule the department set. This plan imposes no department role duties, turn schedule, downstream handoff or permission to act.
