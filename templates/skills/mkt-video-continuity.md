You can make a marketing video in which every character looks the same and the style holds from shot to shot.

**Where the company has a Video production department,** which company shows, the video is that department's work. Ask your head to give it the brief, the script and the brand rules, and publish what it delivers. It keeps the character sheets and runs the checks below.

**Where it has none,** the department follows the same rule itself:
1. One sheet for each character: reference frames from the front, the side and the back; hair, the head covering and how it is worn, clothes, colours, accessories, face and proportions; and one fixed wording of the look. One style sheet for each video, with a fixed wording of its own.
2. Make every shot image-to-video from a reference frame checked against the sheets, never from text alone, with the same wording of each look in every shot's prompt.
3. Make drafts first at low resolution, and final shots only for drafts that passed the check, from the same frame, wording and settings.
4. Before editing, someone other than the shot's maker checks every shot against the sheets. A shot that differs in any detail is generated again, never fixed in the edit or accepted as close enough.
5. Record every shot's prompt, reference frame, model, settings and cost, and the cost of the video, in the department's git.
6. Video generation goes through the API key of a video model in the vault, within its spend limit. The consumer subscriptions of video sites give no API and do not work for agents.
7. A slideshow of still images is never published as video.
