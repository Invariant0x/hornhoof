The head follows the people responsible for the department and owns the brief, budget and final acceptance. Choose the route that fits the result, whether making footage, editing it or recording a screen.

Check which tools the work requires and what the existing account provides. If a service needs credentials, ask the responsible person to add the required sign-in to a named vault entry. The operator reads it and enters it at the protected sign-in surface, keeping it out of prompts, commands, task records and git. Record the spending limit and currency. Use a service cap where possible and stop before the order can exceed its limit. A person pays for credit or signs for the company. The agent keeps its usual subscription. Set up the people and route while missing access is arranged.

A tracker pipeline maps the work. The stage owner assigns each required check and records its owner, input and evidence. If there is no eligible keeper, the stage owner assigns a qualified substitute. Keep the result in progress until the exact version has the required acceptance, even if the tracker can advance without it. Assign acceptance of the probe, the clips, the draft cut and the finished work.

For a long work, make the brief, story, production bible, breakdown and probe once for the work. The bible holds the character sheets, relevant sets and markers, look book, color script and sound palette. Assign scenes as separate tasks that can run in parallel. Give each scene the common references and a keeper. Send changes through the lead keeper and head and pass the agreed revision to every affected scene. Assemble the scene edits into one cut before finishing.

Use one-shot calls for written options such as shot prompts. Give the calls the requirements and the evidence they can read. A visual verdict comes from a checker who opened and played the exact media. For generation, choose text or an accepted starting frame according to what must be held in the shot. Use the tool's controls for action, camera, sound and consistency, and make drafts at the lowest cost that still makes the risks visible.

Check the draft cut before committing resources to final clips. Recheck replacement clips before the editor uses them. Give the colorist, sound designer and editor their finishing duties across the whole cut. For a short video the head may give both finishing duties to the editor when that person can do the work. The quality reviewer checks the finished cut and the head accepts it before mastering. The department delivers the result.

Keep the brief, prompts, reference frames, accepted probe, clip decisions and cost in the department's git. Record tools, charges including retries, total and currency, and the files or their versioned locations.
