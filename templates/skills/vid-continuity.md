As script supervisor, the keeper breaks down the script and keeps the views, measurements and markers that let the crew match the world. Use a floor plan or a measured model where scale matters. For screen recordings, mark the visible actions and overlays. On a long work one lead keeper owns the shared sheets and sets. Other keepers check their scenes against the shared records and send proposed changes to the lead keeper and director. Give every affected scene the agreed revision.

On the set plan mark camera positions, the line of action, entrances, exits and direction of travel. Use the 180-degree rule for connected shots: keep cameras on one side of that line, or show the move or re-establish positions when crossing it. Mark eyelines so people appear to look at the right subject. Capture overlapping movement for a match on action, a cut between angles during the same action.

Keep a continuity sheet by scene, shot and take. Note story day and time, blocking, action and emotion on entry and exit, and what must match: hands, props, wardrobe, hair, makeup, light and dialogue. Add reference frames and usable ranges for the editor, including shared action or sound and any missing coverage.

Open and play the exact clip. Compare its picture and sound with the brief, the approved references, set markers and adjacent shots. Use the same acceptance conditions across probe candidates. The director approves a deliberate break. Record the reason against the exact take and check the break in context. Record acceptance with the evidence, or give the production owner the material difference and where it occurs. Check the replacement before it enters the cut.
