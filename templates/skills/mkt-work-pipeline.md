Use this ability to choose a verification plan for an assigned marketing output or channel. Inputs are its audience, account, content, language, approved approach, risk, channel policy and available check evidence. The task determines the assigned checkers and gates.

1. Describe the channel and output: whom it reaches, account, limits, permitted claims, any approval rule its department set and measures such as reach and complaints. Record missing policy rather than inventing it.
2. Identify irreversible exposure. Public items wait for nobody unless the department set an approval rule for them or its own check stops one; a deletion of a public record for good needs the CEO's yes. Sign-ins stay in the vault, and every post is marked in the department's git at once and committed.
3. Plan deterministic checks for prices, claims and links against the department's own records, "do not write" marks, the platform's limits and valid page markup. Code checks need tests including rejected cases; a missing check remains an explicit gap requiring authorized human assessment.
4. Group reversible internal drafts, campaign preparation or a site audit where shared evidence makes comparison useful. A batch is a way to inspect work, not approval to dispatch it.
5. The first pieces of a new channel get a second read by another employee in a fresh context; rising complaints stop the channel until the head has looked at them.
6. For model checks, require a versioned example set with known answers labeled independently, an owner-set threshold, separate false-accept and false-reject counts, and new examples from real misses. Revalidate when the check, prompt, lists or model changes. Keep company examples private. An unvalidated check, including subjective brand review without a valid set, proves no pass.

Return the checks, evidence, gaps and any approval its department set for this output. This plan does not assign role duties, impose a turn cadence or grant publication rights.

When the output is a video, follow vid-laws. Hand the brief to Video production if the company has it, and keep publication in Marketing. Otherwise name one keeper for the whole video. That keeper checks each draft and final shot against the brief, style and character sheets and measured set before editing. Remake any shot that drifts. Deliver moving video.
