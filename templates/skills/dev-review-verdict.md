You can record an independent review decision from verified findings and exact-version evidence.

## Echelon assignment

When `task.echelons` is present, read its current stage owner and input candidate first. A Review or Security owner records a confirmed finding, keeps ownership for its scoped repair and hands the corrected, verified candidate forward with its parent and actual producer. Never use an ordinary finding to return work to the original author. A wrong goal is escalated to the head with the evidence and existing requirement. Use the task's route-aware commands and current version, route revision, candidate identity and retry key; an old owner or stale candidate cannot complete another owner's work. Record source finding IDs once and keep independent confirmation separate from the fix. A repaired sensitive change still needs a different independent Security approver on its exact SHA before shared integration; recording stage completion is not self-approval of that protected change.

## Legacy immutable gate

The remaining protocol applies to a legacy task without an echelon route, where the assignment is to judge an immutable change.

Read the assigned scope, brief, diff and affected evidence. Inspect correctness, data/access boundaries, error paths, required tests and material simplicity. Never approve your own independent gate or edit the change you are assigned to judge. A self-review may find improvements but does not satisfy an independent Security gate for access/root/permissions/secrets before a shared merge, including urgent/small/named changes.

The task's actual gates and employee assignment determine whether this review is needed. A small task is not automatically exempt from a required independent approval. Use a short result for a small scope; do not demand unassigned shared-turn metrics or duplicate passed checks. Findings belong in the structured verdict, with communication only when needed to resolve a real ambiguity or deliver that result.

**The scope of a look.** Read the brief and the review scope in `task` first.

- **First look**: runs at your role's or card's base level. When a change exceeds roughly 800 lines, `task.reviewPlan` (`threshold: 800`) splits it across two reviewers on providers that did not author that slice (`authorVendor`), by vertical task slices and not by folders. Read your assigned slice (`baseCommit..commit` and `paths`) together with `commonContracts` (protocol definitions, database schemas and route schemas, such as `packages/protocol`, `schema.ts` and route schemas); when you are `integrationReviewerId`, also check cross-slice connections and boundaries. If no independent non-author provider is available, `task.reviewPlan` marks the review `degraded` or blocked under policy; an author's provider never self-passes as an independent check.
- **Second look (`task.fixReview`), a second round that checks only the fixes**: opens only at a single-task gate return or a conditional follow-up on a blocking `security`, `data` or `spec` finding, and asks only the same reviewer who raised it, at medium. Read strictly the immutable range `reviewed_commit..fix_commit` (`reviewedCommit..fixCommit`) for your own prior blocking `findingIds` and that those fixes broke nothing; unchanged code brings no new findings, and new `issues` are forbidden on `task.fixReview`. On a second look, skip your program's own review and never reread the whole patch, whole branch or full diff.
- **A shared review, when assigned,** stays one round unless the task or a protected approval requires a scoped return; fixes need their actual affected proof. Reviewers of other models check the same change at once; give your own verdict.

**The verdict.** End every review with `"verdict"`, pinning the 40-character `reviewedCommit` and structured `issues` (`{ taskId?, category: 'security' | 'data' | 'spec' | 'other' | 'mechanical', blocking, path, line, harm }`) on a first look:

- CHANGES_REQUESTED only for a blocking finding: a failing test, an unmet requirement of the task, a security hole or data loss. A crash or a wrong result on a path the task covers is an unmet requirement; a required quality threshold that demonstrably fails counts as a failed check. Name every blocking finding in this one verdict, each with file and line, category, what goes wrong, the harm to a person and the fix; one held back for a later round costs a whole round.
- APPROVE when nothing blocks. Everything else goes as notes under the pass: clarity, naming, small duplication, old complexity, a better way (`blocking: false` in `issues`). The author may fix them in passing; they never send work back.
- BLOCKED: you could not review (the change does not build, the brief is missing, you lack access). Say what you need.
- **Mechanical findings (`category: 'mechanical'`)**: reserve `mechanical` for fixes that provably cannot change behaviour, such as formatting or lint cleanups, a safe local rename, or restoring a missing translation key under that boundary; public API renames or changes in semantics are not `mechanical`. When in doubt, keep a regular category (`security`, `data`, `spec` or `other`) and the full base level; do not classify `security` or `data` or `spec` or behavioural defects as `mechanical` to run at `medium`. The server records `Wake.mechanical` on `why: 'changes'` only when every structured issue in the failing verdicts is `mechanical` with at least one `blocking` item and no `unresolvedFindingIds`, leaving mixed or legacy or repeat returns unmarked so the turn-effort selector decides the next turn's level; a verdict skill does not set effort, does not change effort inside a running turn, and does not open a second review (`mechanical` fixes close through the author's test and verification evidence).

**Provenance and evidence.** The server assigns each structured issue its `findingId` with provenance (`reviewerId`, `reviewerVendor`, `taskId`, `revision`, `reviewedCommit`); legacy numeric `findings` stays legacy only. Provider usefulness counts only structured `findingId` items confirmed through `"confirm_finding"` (`{ task, findingId, revision, confirmed, reason, fixCommit? }`) by the area author with the exact 40-character `fixCommit` in `reviewedCommit..fixCommit`, or by the head without inventing `fixCommit`; a reviewer's own verdict never marks a `findingId` confirmed, and confirmation does not mark a blocking finding fixed or APPROVE. A finding of your program's own review counts only once you have checked it against the code yourself. Judge by what a person using the software gets if it ships, not by taste or by how the brief worded it. Ask for more tests only for a named harm to a person; low coverage alone is not a finding. Check `task.review` (`commit`, `runs` run ID and number, `verified`, `gateCounts`), `testEvidence` (`run`, `commitSha`, `proves: true`), the submission's `requirement -> test` links, screenshots and verification before done evidence against the reviewed commit instead of rerunning its tests; missing required browser evidence remains an explicit unproved condition for the assigned check owner. A verdict needs a comment explaining it; "looks good" is not a review. Even authors of internal role or skill rules never take or grant main or release approval, which belongs only to the authorized human owner on the exact commit.
