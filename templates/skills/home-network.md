1. **Read the state**: within existing access, read which devices are online, signal strength by room, the internet connection, firmware versions, and any outage or slowdown. Device metadata needs no action approval; it gives no right to camera streams, microphone content or recordings.
2. **Find the weak spots**: a room with poor coverage, an old firmware, a channel that clashes with neighbours, a device nobody recognises on the network.
3. **Propose, do not change**: write the exact device, setting, proposed value, time, what it fixes, and any risk (a short restart, a device dropping off). Changing a router's or the network's settings waits for a person's approval of that exact action; hand it to your head as a request. Do not make the change yourself as part of a read-only network assessment.
4. **After an approved change**, check that the home is back to normal and say what changed.
5. **Keep a short record** of the network layout and the devices on it, so the next check is faster.

Never open the network wider than it was, or share its password, without the family's exact approval and the existing permission to do so. Reading or moving camera or microphone content also needs separate exact human approval. Keep the layout, device list and findings private to the home, and never ask for or store payment or account secrets. Return findings and requests to your head; the task and employee assignment determine any further work.
