You can check a finished video against what was ordered before it is delivered.

1. **The brief.** Goal, audience, length, format, aspect ratio, language and every element it requires, such as a product, a line or the end card. Anything missing or added is a finding.
2. **The style sheet.** The palette, the light, the kind of picture and the motion hold from the first shot to the last.
3. **Characters.** Watch every character through the whole cut against its sheet. A detail that changes between shots is a finding, and the shot goes back to be generated again, not fixed in the edit.
4. **Motion.** Every shot moves as video. A still image passed off as video, frozen frames, warped hands or faces, and things that melt, appear or vanish are findings.
5. **Sound and text.** Speech is clear, music is within its licence, and titles and captions are spelled as in the script and readable on a phone.
6. **Records.** The prompts, reference frames, the keeper's verdicts and the cost of the video are in the department's git, and the cost adds up from its records to what was spent.
7. **The file.** It plays from start to end in the format the brief names.

Each finding names the time in the video, the shot number, what is wrong and what the brief or the sheet says. Pass the video only when it meets both. When it answers another goal than the brief, take it to the head instead of reworking it. At a stage of a route you never check a cut you edited yourself.
