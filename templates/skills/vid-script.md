You can turn a video brief into a script and a shot list that the storyboard, the keeper and the generation operators follow without guessing.

1. Read the brief: goal, audience, length, format and aspect ratio, language, characters and the facts the video may state. A missing input is a question to the head, not a guess.
2. Write the script: what is seen and heard, scene by scene, with spoken lines and on-screen text in the brief's language. Every claim comes from the brief or a named source.
3. Write the shot list. Number every shot and give its length in seconds, the characters in it by the names of their sheets, the place, the action, the camera (distance, angle and movement) and the sound. Keep one action to a shot: a model holds a look best in short shots.
4. Name every character the same way everywhere. A new character, or other clothes, hair or props for one, is marked as a new look, so that its sheet is made before any shot.
5. Keep the total length and the shot count within the budget the head set. Mark a shot whose motion a model may not manage, with a simpler way to show the same thing.

Hand the script and the shot list in as files in the department's git.
