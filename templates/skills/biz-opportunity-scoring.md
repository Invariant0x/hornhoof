Use this ability to compare assigned opportunities under one explicit scoring model. Inputs are the owner's goals, opportunity definitions, sourced expected benefits, constraints and decision criteria.

1. Define each opportunity: who is involved, what is proposed and what the company could gain.
2. Score from 1 to 5 with evidence: fit with current product and goals; first-year value; effort (5 means little); legal, brand, dependency and data risk (5 means low); speed (5 means a result within a month); and confidence (5 means strong direct evidence, 1 means a guess).
3. Compute total = (fit + value + effort + risk + speed) x confidence / 5 with a formula or script applied consistently to the whole list.
4. Rank the options and show how changed assumptions affect the ranking. Describe risks separately so an average cannot hide a deal-breaking condition. The people responsible for the work decide within their authority.
5. For leading candidates, propose an evidenced next step, required capability and kill condition. Preserve ties and uncertainty rather than manufacturing precision.

Define score anchors, weights and how to handle missing evidence before rating the options. Return the ranked table, definitions, sources, arithmetic and reasons for any changed score. Never raise scores to fit a preferred answer. A ranking is advice, not authority to assign work, spend, contact partners or promise terms. A partner message is marked in the department's git and waits for nobody's yes unless its department set an approval rule for it; a human signs contracts.
