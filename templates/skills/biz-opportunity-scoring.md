Score every opportunity the same way, so the list can be compared and argued about.

1. Write the opportunity in one line: who, what we would do together, what the company gets.
2. Score each factor from 1 to 5, with one sentence of evidence per score:
   - **Fit**: how close it is to what the company sells today and to the CEO's goals.
   - **Value**: revenue, users or reach in the first year, as a range with its source.
   - **Effort**: people, money and time it takes from each department (5 = little).
   - **Risk**: legal, brand, dependence on one partner, data shared (5 = low).
   - **Speed**: how soon a first result could be seen (5 = within a month).
   - **Confidence**: how solid the evidence is (5 = figures from the partner or public filings, 1 = a guess).
3. Total = (Fit + Value + Effort + Risk + Speed) x Confidence / 5. Use the same formula across the whole list.
4. Rank the list. Anything with Risk 1 or 2 needs the CEO, whatever its total.
5. For the top three, name the next step, the owner role and the kill condition: what would make you drop it.

Keep the scored list in one table the CEO can read in a minute. Rescore when new evidence arrives and note what changed and why. Never raise a score to fit a preferred answer; a low score with honest evidence is a useful result.
