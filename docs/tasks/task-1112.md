# Department heads and approval defaults

Task: 1112. Kind: known behavior. Risk: access.

## Intended result

A head runs its own department in any running conversation turn with its active named owner's rights, or the active CEO's when no valid department owner is available. A new message from a person is not required. Department boundaries and current permission checks still apply. Ordinary workers get no management authority. The acting head and the person whose rights it used remain in the existing audit records.

The proposed default is `new_kinds`: the first kind of work needs approval; after approval, the same normalized kind on that department's approval channel follows its policy without another question. This is an approval category, not a physical SMTP/webhook account or an automatic template-content comparison. An explicit stored `every` rule and an invalid stored limit still require approval every time. Commit approvals always name an exact commit. No stored policy is migrated or rewritten.

## Architecture

Extend the existing `asOwner` mechanism shared by `decisionPrincipal` and `standingPrincipal`, preserving the orchestrator's current policy. Resolve a head's department and current named owner from the database; check active status, company and department access, then fall back to an active company owner. Keep pending input and stopped turns without authority. Rechecking delegation must detect a changed principal.

## Sources of unnecessary human questions

- Decision and standing context depended on the last human message to the head. Replace this dependency with the current department owner, otherwise CEO.
- Department control descriptions required a person's fresh or earlier words for staffing, products, teams and employee changes. Describe department authority directly.
- Head outbound descriptions required a person's words for channels, limits, autoSend, account author assignment and catalog publication. Use the same department principal and existing own-department guards.
- Approval and wait policy tools described a fresh human turn. State that the head sets its own rules in any turn, under its department restrictions.
- The head skill required a person's words to turn an employee on. State the department rule as the condition.
- Sales and marketing source-publication skills required a fresh human instruction for catalog publication. Route that decision to the head under its department rules.
- The common approval default asked every time. Use new kinds for channels with no stored policy, retaining exact commit approval and explicit stored policies.

Account credentials, consent, suppression, unsubscribe, quotas, price restrictions, required model checks, per-item public outbound approval and the human-controlled autonomous release switch keep their existing rules. A head can decide whether direct-message autoSend is appropriate under its rules; the default approval category does not silently turn an outbound channel's autoSend on. The referenced live questions were not edited or answered by this task.
